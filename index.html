Lakshya A Agrawal

I am a Computer Science PhD student at UC Berkeley, where I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Matei Zaharia and Prof. Dan Klein. I am affiliated with Sky Lab, BAIR, and Berkeley NLP. I build AI systems that improve through experience, by optimizing their prompts, agent harnesses, and model weights. My research spans reflective optimization, reinforcement learning, and continual learning. My background in programming-language tools led to Monitor-Guided Decoding and multilspy at Microsoft Research. MGD built a static-analysis harness around an LLM to guide code generation, letting a smaller model outperform a larger one. At Berkeley, MAST studied how complex LLM systems fail, while LangProBe mapped their cost–quality frontier: across a broad range of tasks, a smaller model with the right harness can outperform a larger one at lower cost.

These results suggested that automatically optimizing harnesses from rich textual feedback could be more sample-efficient than updating model weights. GEPA optimizes prompts by reflecting on execution traces and textual feedback; optimize_anything extends this to the full agent harness; and gskill learns repository-specific skills for coding agents. I then brought this ability to learn in text space into model training. mmGRPO is an RL method for multi-component LLM systems, combining weight updates with prompt optimization. Fast-Slow Training interleaves fast prompt adaptation with slower RL updates, so prompts absorb task-specific lessons while weights need not change as much. This improves data efficiency and performance while preserving the capacity of the model to learn subsequent tasks. I am continuing to study how self-improving AI systems should learn in the harness and in the model.

I am also very grateful for the opportunity to work with Prof. Joseph E. Gonzalez, Prof. Ion Stoica, Prof. Alex Dimakis, and Prof. Omar Khattab, each of whom has shaped how I approach research.

Background

Education & experience

Before Berkeley, I was an AI4Code Research Fellow at Microsoft Research (2022–2024), working with Dr. Aditya Kanade, Dr. Navin Goyal, Dr. Shuvendu Lahiri, and Dr. Sriram Rajamani.

I studied Computer Science and Applied Mathematics at IIIT-Delhi, graduating first in my department. I did my undergraduate thesis with Dr. Raghava Mutharaju, and spent two summers at EPFL with Prof. James Larus.

I also interned as a software development engineer at Microsoft in 2021 and participated in Google Summer of Code in 2019, working on the Maxima-to-Python transpiler.

Teaching & service

I was a teaching assistant for Machine Learning (CSE343/ECE563) and Theory of Computation (CSE322) at IIIT-Delhi in 2021.

I have reviewed for NeurIPS 2025, ICLR 2025, and ISEC 2024, and mentor student collaborators on the GEPA project.

Other open-source contributions include INRIA/spoon, mozilla/bugbug, and memorizing-transformers-pytorch.
