Lakshya A Agrawal

I work on self-improving AI systems: agents that learn continually from their own experience, whether by rewriting their harness or by updating their weights. That spans reflective optimization of the harness around a model, its prompts, skills, and architecture (GEPA, optimize_anything); post-training that trains the harness and the model together (Multi-module GRPO, Fast-Slow Training); understanding how agents fail and behave in production (MAST, LangProBe); and reliability, with correct-by-construction guarantees for LLM-generated code (Monitor-Guided Decoding, multilspy).

About

I am a Computer Science PhD student at UC Berkeley, where I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Matei Zaharia and Prof. Dan Klein, affiliated with the Sky Computing Lab, BAIR, and the Berkeley NLP Group. My research focusses self-improving LLM-based AI systems, which includes post-training and reinforcement learning for language models, and reflective optimization of the harnesses around them, so that AI systems can learn from richer feedback like natural language, environment state, beyond scalar rewards alone. I am very grateful for the opportunity to work with Prof. Ion Stoica, Prof. Joseph Gonzalez, Prof. Alex Dimakis, and Prof. Omar Khattab (MIT), each of whom has shaped how I approach research. My research is supported by an Amazon AI PhD Fellowship and a Laude Institute Open Research Residency.

My work on DSPy led to GEPA (ICLR 2026 Oral), a reflective optimizer that improves agents by reflecting in natural language on their execution traces. With 55M+ downloads,GEPA is now integrated as a core optimizer in Google's Agent Development Kit, DSPy, MLflow, Pydantic AI, Comet Opik and many others, and used in production at more than fifty organizations. optimize_anything generalizes the same loop to any text artifact, from entire agent harnesses to CUDA kernels and scheduling policies. To train the model and its harness together, I co-led mmGRPO, which extends GRPO to multi-module agents and composes it with prompt optimization, and most recently Fast-Slow Training, where fast textual updates and slow weight updates let LLMs adapt continually without forgetting. The Research section below has the details.

Before Berkeley, I was an AI4Code Research Fellow at Microsoft Research (2022–2024), where I worked with Dr. Aditya Kanade, Dr. Navin Goyal, Dr. Shuvendu Lahiri, and Dr. Sriram Rajamani on the correctness of LLM-generated code, which led to Monitor-Guided Decoding (NeurIPS 2023) and multilspy. I studied Computer Science and Applied Mathematics at IIIT-Delhi, graduating first in my department, and spent those years on language runtimes and transpilers: a Google Summer of Code project now shipped with Maxima, the GraalVM runtime for the CAL dataflow language with Prof. James Larus at EPFL, and my thesis with Dr. Raghava Mutharaju (more under Programming languages and systems).

I am passionate about open source software and about creating useful artifacts from my research; every project above is open source. I look forward to learning about the use cases and failure modes of these AI systems, which helps me articulate my research problems. For more details, see my CV. Research

Five threads: harness optimization, post-training, understanding agents, AI for code, and the programming-languages foundations under all of it.

01Harness optimization

Prompts, skills, contexts, and agent architectures are the harness around a model. I build optimizers that improve all of them by reflecting on execution traces in language rather than compressing them into a reward. gskill applies the same machinery to coding agents.

Reflective optimization

GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning

Learning from language, not just rewards.

Given any AI system with one or more LLM prompts, GEPA samples full trajectories, reflects on them in natural language to diagnose what went wrong, proposes and tests prompt updates, and combines complementary lessons from the Pareto frontier of its own attempts. Because each rollout yields a paragraph of diagnosis rather than a single scalar, GEPA learns from a handful of examples where RL needs thousands.

ICLR 2026 Oral 6.4k GitHub stars Integrated in DSPy, MLflow, Pydantic AI, Google ADK, Comet Opik
Paper Code Docs ICLR talk Blog

Production write-ups from Databricks (open models optimized with GEPA beat Claude Opus 4.1 at ~90× lower cost), Dropbox, and Decagon; an OpenAI Cookbook tutorial on self-evolving agents, written with Bain & Company; and adoption at Shopify, Uber, Meta, and AWS, among others. Microsoft AI used GEPA-optimized judges to filter pre-training data for MAI-Thinking-1, Nous Research used it for agent self-evolution and skill learning, and AI-control researchers used it to find monitors that block 90% of malicious code at a 1% audit budget. Universal text optimization

optimize_anything

If you can measure it, you can optimize it.

Prompts hold no special status. Anything expressible as text with an evaluator (code, CUDA kernels, agent architectures, scheduling policies, SVGs, mathematical constructions) can be improved by the same reflect-mutate-select loop. A single optimizer reaches state of the art across six unrelated domains, taking an ARC-AGI agent from 32.5% to 89.5% and cutting cloud-scheduling costs by about 40%. In July 2026 optimize_anything became engine-pluggable: one call dispatches the same task to GEPA, AutoResearch, or Meta-Harness, and the engines compose into pipelines. omni, a meta-optimizer that explores with all three and continues from the best, beats every standalone optimizer on Frontier-CS under a matched budget.

02Post-training and continual learning

Reflection in the harness adapts fast but is capped by the base model; RL changes the model but is slow and forgets. Training the harness and the weights together is where I think post-training is headed.

RL for modular agents

Multi-module GRPO & dspy.GRPO

Policy gradients for compound AI systems.

dspy.GRPO is the first GRPO pipeline for tuning modular agents, including compound AI systems that compose multiple structured LM calls and tool invocations. A server-client abstraction decouples policy-gradient updates from multi-stage agentic rollouts. Multi-module GRPO then composes RL with prompt optimization, gaining about 11% on average over either alone and showing that the two are complementary rather than competing.

Continual learning

Fast-Slow Training

Fast weights in the harness, slow weights in the model.

RL bakes every lesson into parameters and forgets what it knew. Prompt optimization adapts instantly but plateaus at the base model's capability. FST interleaves the two: GEPA-style reflection updates the harness quickly while policy-gradient updates consolidate what proves durable, sampling rollouts from a Pareto frontier of prompts rather than a single one. The result is about 3× more sample-efficient than RL with about 70% less drift from the base model.

03Understanding and evaluating language models and agents

You cannot optimize what you have not characterized. This thread measures how language programs and agents actually behave, in controlled benchmarks and in production.

Failures and deployments

Why agents fail, and how they behave in production

You can't optimize what you haven't characterized.

MAST is the first taxonomy of multi-agent failures: 14 modes in 3 categories, distilled from 1,000+ annotated traces across 7 frameworks. Measuring Agents in Production then studies how deployed agents actually behave, across real production systems.

Benchmarking language programs

LangProBe: A Language Programs Benchmark

What do architectures and optimizers actually buy you?

The first large-scale benchmark for language programs: over 2,000 combinations of tasks, program architectures, prompt optimizers, and models. LangProBe gave the first systematic study of how architectures and optimizers, alone and composed, trade quality against cost, and showed that optimized programs offer a strong cost–quality Pareto improvement over raw model calls, while human judgment about which compositions to pursue still matters.

04AI for code

Code is where I test these ideas against reality: grounding generation in program analysis so models stop hallucinating APIs, and teaching agents a codebase once so they stop relearning it.

Coding agents

gskill: Automatically Learning Skills for Coding Agents

Teach the agent your codebase, once.

Given a repository, gskill generates its own evaluation tasks, then uses optimize_anything to learn skill files that capture how the code is organized, built, and tested. Skills learned with a cheap model transfer to production agents: in our evaluation they took GPT-5-mini from 24% to 93% and Claude Code from 79% to 100%, while making it 47% faster.

Trustworthy code generation

Monitor-Guided Decoding & multilspy

Static analysis inside the decoding loop.

MGD puts static analysis inside the decoding loop: through the Language Server Protocol it monitors generation and, the moment analysis flags an issue, corrects it in a single pass with no rejection sampling. The generated code is correct by construction with respect to the analysis: no hallucinated symbols, methods called in the right order and with the right arguments, and fewer compilation, runtime, and security errors at minimal overhead. With MGD, even a 350M-parameter model produces more compilable code than a 175B one. multilspy, the LSP client built for it, is now the standard way coding agents talk to language servers.

From Prompts to Programs: Text Optimization with GEPA and DSPy

Self-Improvement of Context, Harness, and Model Weights through Reflective Optimization
Jul 2026
Main stage, AI Engineer World's Fair 2026, Moscone West, San Francisco
Abstract

Large language models are increasingly adapted to downstream tasks via reinforcement learning methods like GRPO, which often require thousands of rollouts to learn new tasks. We argue that language provides a much richer learning medium: an LLM can reflect on full trajectories (including reasoning, tool calls and errors) to diagnose failures and propose targeted improvements. We introduce GEPA, a reflective prompt optimizer that incorporates this principle, outperforming GRPO by up to 20% while using up to 35× fewer rollouts across tasks spanning 5+ domains, and also works with black-box models.

Building on this, we introduce optimize_anything, a unified API that generalizes reflective optimization to arbitrary text parameters. This single system achieves state-of-the-art results across eight fundamentally different areas, including nearly tripling ARC-AGI accuracy via agent architecture discovery, generating CUDA kernels that beat PyTorch, and cutting cloud scheduling costs by 40% through policy discovery, establishing LLM-based reflective search as a general-purpose problem-solving paradigm.

Finally, I present Fast-Slow Training (FST), which brings reflective optimization into LLM post-training. FST jointly optimizes model parameters ("slow weights") via RL and textual contexts ("fast weights") via GEPA. Because the fast channel quickly absorbs task-specific nuances, the slow parametric updates are freed to consolidate general reasoning rather than memorizing task details. This yields up to 3× better sample efficiency and a higher performance asymptote with significantly lower drift from the base model. This reduced drift preserves plasticity for continual learning, allowing FST to adapt sequentially where parameter-only RL stalls.

Broadly, our work advocates a fundamental shift in AI adaptation: replacing task-specific algorithms with diagnostic evaluation, and evolving from parameter-only post-training to the joint optimization of prompts, agent architectures, and model weights.
